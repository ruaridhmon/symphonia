Symphonia synthetic evaluation protocol
Practical instructions for generating panels, running the experiment and producing the figures
Purpose. Test whether Symphonia preserves claims, agreement, disagreement and uncertainty through its three-round process. Test whether information loss changes a decision. All participant contributions and semantic evaluations are generated by LLMs; reference counts, scoring and constructed decision outcomes are calculated in code.
Success means accurate representation of the evidence and the panel. Increased agreement is not automatically a successful result. This study evaluates a synthetic setting; it does not establish how human experts would behave.
1 Set up the study and run a small pilot
Use the settings below for the pilot. Debug the workflow on these cases, then freeze the prompts, scoring rules and model settings before generating the main test set.
Setting | Pilot | Main study starting specification
Policy settings | Six settings listed in step 2 | The same six settings
Independent evidence maps | Two per setting = 12 | 20 per setting = 120
Claims per map | 15 | 15, with a larger-claim stress test
Panel sizes | 8 and 32 | 8, 32 and 64
Summary length | 500 words | 250, 500 and 1,000 words
Repeated model runs | Two per cell | Three per cell
Interactive experiment | Six maps, 8 participants | 30 balanced maps, 32 participants

These main-study numbers are planning defaults. Use pilot variation to choose the final number of independent maps for a prespecified meaningful effect. If resources are limited, reduce experimental cells before sacrificing independent scenarios.
Set up access to Symphonia, its underlying model and at least three model families across generation and evaluation. Keep the summarising model identical across competing workflows in the core comparison.
Assign separate roles: scenario generator, participant generator, summarising system, semantic evaluators and decision readers. Save exact model identifiers, settings, prompts and all responses.
Implement Experiment A first. It measures information preservation with fixed participant responses. Add Experiment B to measure what feedback does to subsequent judgments.
Pilot deliverable: 12 validated cases, complete logs, functioning automated scores and draft versions of the five figures. Do not include pilot cases in the confirmatory test set.

2 Create the reference cases
Use six fictional policy settings: school attendance support, school inclusion, school health hubs, preventive healthcare, youth diversion and support after release from custody. Use invented places and evidence so that outside factual knowledge is not required.
For each map, ask an LLM to propose a policy question and 15 atomic claims. An atomic claim expresses one proposition with an explicit population, condition and timeframe. Store the map as JSON. Validate numerical relationships and consistency in code.
Include in every map | Construction instruction
Agreement | Include clearly supported and clearly opposed propositions.
Confident disagreement | Give two groups opposing positions with high confidence.
Insufficient evidence | Make the supplied evidence unable to settle at least one proposition.
Consequential minority | Give a small minority a supported qualification that changes a defined decision.
Unsupported minority | Include dissent without reliable support; it must not become established fact.
Context and dependence | Include apparently conflicting claims about different populations, and multiple participants citing one source.
Shared error and values | Include one popular but false empirical claim and one unresolved normative disagreement.

Vary the evidence graph, numerical values, source quality, qualifying conditions and distributions across maps. A paraphrase of the same evidence structure is a repeat, not a new independent map. Mix these features across the 15 claims; they need not occupy separate claims.
Keep three separate records
World record: constructed evidence, source links and empirical truth where defined. Allow unresolved claims. Do not assign objective truth to value judgments.
Panel record: each participant’s private evidence and intended stance, plus what they actually say at every round. Keep spontaneous mention separate from agreement.
Decision record: options, explicit objectives, loss values and the best choice under eligible evidence. Use this only for the decision subset in step 9.
Prevent reference leakage
Give each participant only their assigned evidence. Give each tested method only the inputs available at that stage. Keep hidden truth labels, importance labels, correct summary labels and scoring metadata away from participants and summarising systems.
Assign stable claim and source IDs for scoring. The round-one extractor must find claims in natural language without being handed the gold claim list. Later-round claim IDs must trace back to the propositions actually presented.
Deliverable: validated world, panel and decision records for each scenario, with a log of rejected or repaired cases.

3 Generate and validate the expert contributions
Create each participant in a fresh context. Give them a private evidence packet, stance assignments and a writing-style instruction. Do not show any peer contribution in round one.
Ask for 150–250 words per first-round contribution. Vary prose style and order independently of stance. Record exactly which claims and evidence each participant expresses.
In rounds two and three, record support, oppose or insufficient evidence; confidence from 0 to 100; a short reason; and supporting source IDs. Distinguish a missing answer from insufficient evidence.
On factual claims only, also elicit a probability from 0 to 1 that the proposition is true. This additional probability is needed for the uncertainty analysis. Do not infer it from generic confidence.
Use two separate evaluator models to check that each contribution expresses its assigned content without invented evidence. Validate source IDs and numeric fields in code. Allow one regeneration; otherwise log the failure and replace the case under a fixed rule.
For an ambiguous semantic check, retain both evaluations and mark the disputed field. Do not force agreement by repeatedly prompting until the desired answer appears. Score against validated realised content, not an assignment the generated text failed to express.
4 Set up the comparison methods
Method | Run instruction
Direct synthesis | Ask the common model to summarise the eligible inputs in one pass.
Structured synthesis | Use one pass but explicitly require agreement, dissent, uncertainty, conditions, confidence and provenance.
Staged synthesis | Extract claims, aggregate positions, then write the summary using the common model.
Symphonia | Run the implemented extraction, evaluation, reconsideration and final synthesis workflow.
Reference-fed synthesis | Give the common model the exact eligible claim and vote table. Use this as a diagnostic reference.

Require the same output from all methods: the word-limited narrative plus an audit table of claims, positions, counts, qualifications and sources. Cap the audit table identically across methods; report its token count. Score narrative fidelity separately from audit-table accuracy so a correct table cannot hide misleading prose.
For compression figures, the narrative alone is the reader-facing output. Use the audit table for diagnostic scoring. If testing a combined narrative-and-table product, label that analysis separately and count the full communication budget.
Use equal eligible information for each comparison. Also run a processing-budget comparison in which methods receive the same total token allowance. Record actual tokens, cost, latency and any truncation or failure.

5 Run Experiment A with fixed judgments
This is the primary test of whether Symphonia preserves information. Hold participants’ substantive responses fixed so that differences can be attributed to the processing workflow.
Generate a complete reference response table for rounds two and three before testing the methods. Include both unchanged views and prespecified changes justified by additional evidence.
Run each method’s round-one extraction on the same independent contributions. Match the extracted propositions to the reference with blinded semantic evaluators; retain omissions, merges, splits and changed meanings as errors.
For the controlled full workflow, replay fixed responses only when a displayed proposition matches a reference proposition. If a proposition has changed meaning, return “cannot evaluate as stated” and preserve that mismatch in the log. Never attach a reference vote to a different claim.
Score the feedback shown to participants against the eligible reference at that round. Then score the final output against the actual fixed responses received, while also counting original claims lost upstream.
Run a separate final-summarisation comparison: give every method the identical complete three-round transcript, then compare their final summaries. This isolates summarisation from access to different claims.
Save each intermediate output. Classify an error by the first stage at which it appears and record whether it is repaired later. Do not add stage error percentages together as if they were independent.
6 Run Experiment B with responsive participants
Use a balanced subset of maps. Clone the same initial panel and private evidence into four feedback arms: Symphonia feedback, an exact reference display, a conventional structured LLM summary, and no peer feedback. Use the same initial claim list in this feedback-isolation experiment.
Run round two: participants evaluate the common claim list independently. Construct each arm’s feedback from that arm’s actual responses and eligible evidence.
Run round three: show the assigned feedback, ask for a final stance, confidence and reason, and record any newly used evidence. The no-feedback arm reconsiders its own material for the same number of rounds.
First use a controlled factual subset: assign a prior and likelihood ratios to independent evidence items, update odds by multiplying those ratios, and count shared-source evidence once. Code calculates the update; the LLM expresses it. Freeze normative preferences.
Then repeat with freely responding LLM participants. Give them the same evidence and ask them to revise only for reasons they can identify. Compare results across participant model families.
Measure changes in accuracy, uncertainty, disagreement and decision loss. Compare Symphonia with the exact feedback arm to identify presentation effects, and with no feedback to identify the overall effect of interaction. Score the final summary against each arm’s actual round-three panel.
A later full-workflow interactive test may allow each method to extract its own claim list. Label it separately because extraction and feedback effects are then combined.

7 Score the outputs automatically
Use code for counts, source IDs, distributions and decision losses. Use blinded LLM evaluators for meaning, qualifications and whether the narrative matches the audit table. Apply the definitions on page 8.
Primary outcomes
Decision-critical claim fidelity: the fraction of prespecified consequential claims retained with the correct meaning, conditions, panel status and evidential status. An omitted claim scores zero.
False-consensus rate: the fraction of reference claims without support or opposition consensus that the narrative incorrectly presents as having such consensus. Report fabricated unanimous judgments separately.
Use 80% of valid evaluations in one direction as the primary operational threshold. Require at least 80% panel response coverage. “Insufficient evidence” is a valid evaluation; a missing answer is not. Repeat at 70% and 90%. These thresholds are experimental definitions, not established universal standards.
Secondary outcomes
Measure | What to calculate
Coverage and unsupported assertions | Reference claims retained; output assertions unsupported by eligible inputs.
Meaning and qualification errors | Reversed direction, changed population or timeframe, missing conditions.
Panel distribution error | Jensen–Shannon divergence and absolute support-count errors.
Minority and uncertainty fidelity | Retention of dissent and unresolved evidence with their correct status.
Relations and provenance | Correct contradiction, qualification and source-dependence links.
Truth and calibration | Accuracy and Brier scores on factual claims with known synthetic truth.
Practical performance | Decision regret, tokens, cost, latency and failed runs.

Check the evaluators before relying on them
Create 100 known corruptions and 100 meaning-preserving edits of reference summaries. Balance corruptions across polarity reversal, missing conditions, exaggerated support, invented sources and deleted minority warnings. Have each evaluator identify the changed claim and error without seeing the edit label.
Report detection sensitivity and false-positive rate by error type. Set the acceptance rule in the pilot; a suggested engineering target is at least 90% sensitivity and at most 5% false positives. If an evaluator fails, revise or replace it before freezing the pipeline; disclose unresolved limitations.
Use three model families for final semantic scoring, with majority votes as the primary coding rule. Also report each evaluator separately and conservative bounds for disputed fields. A model council does not eliminate shared bias.

8 Run the robustness checks
Apply each change to the same underlying panel, one at a time. Use all 12 pilot maps and a balanced, prespecified subset of at least 30 main maps. Use three independent reorderings or paraphrases per map. Freeze perturbation selection before examining results.
Change | Expected behaviour
Reorder or paraphrase inputs | Claim meaning and represented panel support remain stable.
Change verbosity or irrelevant prestige labels | Evidential treatment remains stable when substantive information is unchanged.
Repeat a claim within one response | Support counts do not increase.
Add supporters citing a shared source | Participant support increases; independent source count does not.
Add reliable contradictory evidence | Evidence assessment responds to the changed information.
Remove a consequential observation | The corresponding qualification or decision changes when warranted.
Vary minority prevalence | Retain supported qualifications even when only one participant mentions them.

Test minorities of one participant, 12.5% and 25% of the panel. Report absolute counts and proportions. Keep evidence strength fixed while changing prevalence. Report claim-status flips, meaning errors and decision changes; stable wording alone is not the objective.
Repeat selected runs after removing one Symphonia component at a time: claim correction, confidence display, provenance or final reconsideration. Match eligible information and processing budgets where possible. These ablations identify which components contribute to performance.
9 Test whether information loss changes decisions
On a balanced subset, specify a decision rule and losses before generating summaries. Give three separate reader models only the narrative, the policy options and the decision objective. Ask for an action and short justification. Readers must not see the reference map or audit table.
Example. Use actions “universal rollout”, “targeted rollout” and “defer”. In a constructed case with a supported subgroup-harm warning, assign losses of 100, 10 and 30 respectively. In a matched case without that harm, assign losses of 0, 10 and 30. These are illustrative benchmark units, not empirical estimates. Put the evidence needed to distinguish the cases in the participant inputs.
Calculate regret as the chosen action’s loss minus the lowest loss under the complete eligible evidence. Repeat using a deterministic rule applied to independently decoded summary facts. Include weak or incorrect minority warnings so the benchmark does not reward accepting every dissenting view.
For value-sensitive decisions, repeat across explicitly stated objectives. Report conclusions conditional on those objectives. Never treat one chosen value system as objective policy truth.

10 Analyse and produce the figures
Freeze the main study after the pilot. Prespecify the primary comparator as structured synthesis, the two primary outcomes, the minimum worthwhile effects and the analysis. Keep development, pilot and test scenarios separate.
Compare methods on paired scenarios. Average repeated runs within each scenario and condition before calculating overall effects. Give each independent scenario equal weight.
Calculate 95% confidence intervals with 2,000 bootstrap resamples of independent scenario maps, stratified by policy setting. Keep each map’s panels, perturbations and repeated runs together. Do not treat individual claims as independent replicates.
Adjust the two primary hypothesis tests using Holm’s procedure. Report effect sizes and confidence intervals, not only p-values. Treat the remaining metrics and subgroup comparisons as secondary unless separately preregistered.
Report all scheduled runs. Allow one retry for a technical API failure with the same settings, log both attempts, and retain unresolved failures. Give successful-run scores and a separate conservative analysis counting missing outputs as failed fidelity; report failure rates prominently.
Replicate the core comparison with a second summarising model family. Rotate participant and evaluator families on a prespecified subset. Report scenario-level results and the worst-performing conditions alongside averages.
Figure | Plot specification
1 Information loss by stage | Plot omission and distortion rates after extraction, feedback and final synthesis, separated by method. Include a compact workflow panel.
2 Fidelity under compression | Plot distortion against 250, 500 and 1,000 words; add critical-claim fidelity and false consensus against panel size. Use paired confidence intervals.
3 Disagreement and uncertainty | Plot true versus reported stance proportions; add a status confusion matrix and individual-uncertainty versus between-participant-disagreement plots.
4 Consequential minority evidence | Plot fidelity against minority prevalence, and decision regret for supported, unsupported and absent minority evidence.
5 Robustness and mechanism | Plot paired perturbation effects and component-removal effects. Add model-family replication and cost versus fidelity.

11 Package the results
Save the frozen specification, generation and scoring scripts, prompts, model identifiers, reference maps, complete transcripts, semantic labels, errors, run-level metrics and figure data. Preserve the mapping from every plotted point to its original runs.
Write the results around what was preserved, where distortions arose, whether decisions changed and which components helped. State explicitly that the conclusions concern synthetic participants. No empirical results or performance claims should be written before the experiment is run.

Scoring instructions for implementation
Use these definitions consistently in code. Report narrative and audit-table results separately. Align claims semantically before scoring; do not rely only on supplied IDs. Keep split and merged claims linked to the original propositions.
Claim fidelity and consensus
For each reference claim, record five binary fields: present, meaning correct, conditions correct, panel status correct, and evidential status correct. Strict fidelity is one only when all five pass. A missing claim receives zero. The primary critical-claim score is the mean over the predefined consequential subset; also report all-claim fidelity.
For support and opposition proportions, divide by all valid evaluations, including insufficient-evidence responses. Apply the response-coverage rule first, then the consensus threshold. Also flag insufficient evidence when at least half of valid responses use that option, and substantive disagreement when support and opposition each reach 25%. These latter flags can coexist. Preserve the full distribution regardless of labels.
Count false consensus when a reference claim lacks directional consensus but the output says the panel agrees or rejects it. Report missed genuine consensus as a separate rate. Unsupported new claims are evaluated separately and cannot improve reference-claim recall.
Distribution distortion
For each retained claim, create two three-element probability vectors in the order support, oppose, insufficient evidence: the actual panel distribution and the reported distribution. Compare them with Jensen–Shannon divergence using base-two logarithms. The value is zero for identical distributions and at most one. If using scipy.spatial.distance.jensenshannon, square its returned distance to obtain divergence.
If numeric proportions cannot be recovered, mark distribution reporting as missing rather than inventing values from words such as “many”. Report mean divergence among assessable claims and the assessable fraction. For an all-claim distortion score, assign one to omitted or unassessable claims and otherwise use their divergence; average over all eligible reference claims. Repeat with missingness penalties of 0.5 and 0.75. Label this a composite distortion score, not pure divergence.
Uncertainty and calibration
For the factual subset, calculate binary entropy of each participant’s probability of truth: multiply each of the two probabilities by its base-two logarithm, sum, and negate. Define a zero-probability term as zero. Mean individual entropy measures within-participant uncertainty. Entropy of the mean probability minus mean individual entropy measures between-participant dispersion. Do not substitute confidence ratings for probabilities.
To test whether summaries preserve that distinction, require the audit table to report mean probability, mean individual entropy and between-participant dispersion where the inputs support calculation. Independently code whether the narrative describes uncertainty, disagreement or both. Compare with the reference values and labels.
For claims with binary truth labels, the Brier score is the mean squared difference between a stated probability of truth and the truth label. Use ten fixed probability bins for a reliability plot, show bin counts, and cluster uncertainty by scenario. Do not score empirical calibration on normative or unresolved claims.

Prompt recipes and required files
Use the recipes below as starting instructions. Add the required JSON schema, test on the pilot, then freeze exact wording. Separate untrusted participant text from system instructions.
Participant generation
“You are participant [ID] in a fictional policy exercise. Use only your private evidence packet. Write an independent first-round contribution of 150–250 words expressing the assigned positions and qualifications. Do not invent sources or describe other participants. Return your contribution and a private audit of claims expressed. The private audit will not be shown to the summarising system.”
Claim evaluation and reconsideration
“Evaluate each displayed proposition as written. Return its ID, support/oppose/insufficient evidence, confidence from 0 to 100, a reason and source IDs. On the designated factual subset, also return a probability that the proposition is true. If feedback is supplied, explain any change from your previous response using evidence available to you. If a proposition cannot be evaluated as written, flag it explicitly.”
Common summary instruction
“Summarise only the eligible transcript within [word limit]. Preserve agreement, disagreement, insufficient evidence, consequential minority observations, conditions and source dependence. Distinguish mention frequency from evaluated agreement and confidence from truth. Do not infer agreement from silence. Return the narrative and the specified audit table. Do not add external evidence.”
Blinded semantic evaluation
“Compare this anonymous output with the eligible reference. Match every reference proposition to its output span or mark it omitted. Label meaning, conditions, panel status and evidential status as correct, incorrect or ambiguous. Identify unsupported output assertions. Cite the relevant input and output spans. Do not reward style, length or agreement with your own policy preferences.”
File | Minimum content
study_spec.json | Study version, seeds, conditions, models, prompts, budgets, thresholds, endpoints and analysis choices.
reference.jsonl | Scenario and claim IDs; claim type and scope; evidence and source links; truth where known; decision losses and importance labels.
participants.jsonl | Scenario, panel and participant IDs; private packet; assigned and realised positions; generated text and validation status.
runs.jsonl | Run, scenario, method, arm, stage and repetition IDs; complete input/output; model settings; tokens, time, cost and errors.
scores.jsonl | Claim alignment, evaluator labels and evidence spans; automatic scores; omissions, disagreements and missingness.
figures and report | Five figures, underlying data, evaluator validation, exclusions, sensitivity analyses and the final interpretation.
